Green (Light)

Retired

1936–1951

Introduced at the annual Pottery and Glass Exhibit in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania in January 1936 - meet Light Green. One of the original colors of Fiesta drawn up by Frederick Hurten Rhead himself - Light Green is one of four shades produced during the original run, but that doesn't mean it wasn't a fan favorite!

Notes on Green (Light)

Light Green was in the original 1936 five, and it's the ancestor of what became Fiesta's biggest family — thirteen greens have followed it. It's a mid-depth green with a little blue in it, which is why it sits so comfortably next to Cobalt. Fifteen years on the shelf, then retired in 1951 in the same clear-out that took Cobalt and Old Ivory. Worth knowing if you're identifying a piece: it's close enough to modern Jade to confuse the two at a glance, though Jade is noticeably darker in hand. The name is a period one — nothing to do with the later Medium Green, which is a different and much scarcer color.

Using Green (Light) on a table

Green (Light) has enough color to be noticed without taking over, which makes it a good second or third piece — a salad plate or a serving bowl rather than the whole setting. Its mid lightness (L* 38) means most food reads against it without either washing out or vanishing. It separates strongly from a white cloth (6.9:1) and less so from dark wood.
