Turquoise

Retired

1937-1969

Following the successful release of the 5 originals, Frederick Hurten Rhead introduced Turquoise to add a sense of flair and playfulness to the growing Fiesta family. Turquoise (a shade of teal) would later be revived post-86 and is one of the most popular Fiesta colors ever.

Notes on Turquoise

Turquoise wasn't in the original launch - Rhead added it in 1937, a year after the first five, once it was clear Fiesta was going to be a hit. It's a soft blue-green with far less punch than Cobalt, and it was there to lighten the palette. It worked: thirty-two years on the shelf, retired in 1969, and then brought back after the 1986 relaunch. That makes Turquoise the only Fiesta color made in both eras that's still in production today. The vintage and Post 86 versions are close but not identical, and since both are common, it's one of the more useful pairs to learn to tell apart by hand.

Using Turquoise on a table

Turquoise has enough color to be noticed without taking over, which makes it a good second or third piece - a salad plate or a serving bowl rather than the whole setting. Its mid lightness (L* 67) means most food reads against it without either washing out or vanishing. It nearly disappears against a white cloth (2.5:1), so it shows best on dark wood or a colored runner.

Where Turquoise sits in the line

Turquoise arrived while Blue (Cobalt) was still in production, and is noticeably lighter than it - a complete change of direction rather than a reformulation (ΔE 50.5).
