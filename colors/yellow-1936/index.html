Yellow

Retired

1936-1969

Introduced at the annual Pottery and Glass Exhibit in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania in January 1936 - meet Yellow. One of the original colors of Fiesta drawn up by Frederick Hurten Rhead himself - Yellow is a whimsical color made to bring a sense of joy to every table!

Notes on Yellow

Of the original five, Yellow lasted the longest - thirty-three years, from the 1936 launch right through to 1969, which makes it one of the most common vintage colors you'll come across. It's a warmer, more mustard yellow than anything Fiesta makes now; put it next to Daffodil and the modern one looks almost neon. That longevity is the useful part for a collector: Yellow overlapped nearly every vintage color that ever existed, so it's the safe piece to build a mixed vintage table around. It was retired in 1969 alongside Turquoise, leaving Antique Gold to carry yellow for the line's final three years.

Using Yellow on a table

Yellow carries enough color to lead a table on its own. Use it for the plates and let quieter pieces do the rest; two or three settings of it go a long way. Its mid lightness (L* 73) means most food reads against it without either washing out or vanishing. It nearly disappears against a white cloth (2.1:1), so it shows best on dark wood or a colored runner.

Where Yellow sits in the line

Yellow was the first yellow Fiesta made; 5 more have followed it. It has been in production 33 years against a median of 9 for its era - one of the colors the company kept. In 1936 it arrived alongside Blue (Cobalt), Green (Light), Old Ivory and Red, 1936-1943. What else happened in 1936 →
