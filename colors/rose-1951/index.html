Rose

Retired

1951-1959

Produced from 1951 to 1959, Fiesta's Rose is a shade of pink that is associated with love, romance, and femininity. It is a delicate and soft color that is often used in flowers, particularly roses, which is how the color got its name.

Notes on Rose

Vintage Rose is a soft, dusty pink - much closer to salmon than to anything shocking - and it arrived in 1951 as part of the mid-century trio with Gray and Chartreuse. Eight years, retired 1959. It reads as unmistakably fifties, and that's most of its appeal now. The name was reused for a Post 86 color in 1987 that ran nearly twenty years, so listings need reading carefully: if it says Rose and the seller says vintage, the years are what settle it. Modern Peony is the closest thing currently in production, and it's close enough that the two mix on a table without looking like a mistake.

Using Rose on a table

Rose has enough color to be noticed without taking over, which makes it a good second or third piece - a salad plate or a serving bowl rather than the whole setting. Its mid lightness (L* 68) means most food reads against it without either washing out or vanishing. It nearly disappears against a white cloth (2.4:1), so it shows best on dark wood or a colored runner.

Where Rose sits in the line

Rose took over from Red, 1936-1943 as Fiesta's red, and is lighter and more muted than it - a complete change of direction rather than a reformulation (ΔE 25.7). In 1951 it arrived alongside Chartreuse, 1951-1959, Forest Green and Gray and took the place of Blue (Cobalt), Green (Light) and Old Ivory on the shelf. What else happened in 1951 →
