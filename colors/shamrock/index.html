Shamrock adds a lively and festive touch to any tablescape. Notes on Shamrock

Shamrock ran nineteen years, 2002 to 2021, and it's one of the most saturated greens Fiesta has ever made - a clean, bright grass green with nothing muddy about it. It took over from the acid Post 86 Chartreuse and pushed Yellow off the shelf the year it arrived. It's a lead color: strong enough to carry a table on its own, and strong enough to overwhelm a delicate one. It sits close to the vintage Medium Green, which makes it a genuinely useful modern stand-in for anyone building a vintage-look table without vintage prices.

Using Shamrock on a table

Shamrock carries enough color to lead a table on its own. Use it for the plates and let quieter pieces do the rest; two or three settings of it go a long way. Its mid lightness (L* 62) means most food reads against it without either washing out or vanishing. It nearly disappears against a white cloth (3.0:1), so it shows best on dark wood or a colored runner.

Where Shamrock sits in the line

Shamrock took over from Chartreuse, 1997-1999 as Fiesta's green, and is noticeably darker than it - a clearly different glaze rather than a reformulation (ΔE 14.6). It has been in production 19 years against a median of 7 for its era - one of the colors the company kept. In 2002 it took the place of Yellow, 1987-2002 on the shelf. What else happened in 2002 →
