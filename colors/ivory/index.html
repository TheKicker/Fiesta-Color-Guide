Ivory

Retired

2008–2024 · Color No. 330

Introducing Ivory, made by The Fiesta Tableware Company from 2008 to 2024. It's a classic and timeless shade of off-white, reminiscent of the smooth texture of ivory. Ivory adds an understated elegance to any tablescape. Mix and match Ivory with colors like Scarlet and Sunflower for a sophisticated and vibrant table setting, or pair it with Plum and Slate for a more subdued yet refined combination.

Notes on Ivory

Ivory ran sixteen years, 2008 to 2024, which makes it one of the most common retired colors — you'll have no trouble finding it. It's a warm cream, distinctly yellower than White, and it was the everyday neutral for most of the 2010s. It's also the middle of a confusing trio: Old Ivory from 1936, Ivory from 2008, and Linen from 2025 all sit within a few ΔE of each other. Linen, which arrived the year after Ivory retired, is effectively its replacement and is slightly cooler. If you have Ivory and want to extend a set, Linen is the natural continuation.

Using Ivory on a table

Ivory is soft enough to sit beside almost anything. It works as a bridge between two stronger colors that would otherwise argue. At L* 92 it is one of the paler glazes in the line: dark food reads sharply against it, pale food much less so. It nearly disappears against a white cloth (1.2:1), so it shows best on dark wood or a colored runner.

Where Ivory sits in the line

Ivory took over from Pearl Gray as Fiesta's white, and is lighter and more vivid than it — a clearly different glaze rather than a reformulation (ΔE 16.3). It has been in production 16 years against a median of 7 for its era — one of the colors the company kept. In 2008 it arrived alongside Chocolate and Marigold and took the place of Persimmon and Heather on the shelf. What else happened in 2008 →
