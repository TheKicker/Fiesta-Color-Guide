Chartreuse

Retired

1997-1999 · Color No. 117

Introducing Chartreuse, made by The Fiesta Tableware Company from 1997 to 1999. It's a vibrant and refreshing shade of green with yellow undertones, reminiscent of the citrusy hue of chartreuse liqueur. Chartreuse adds a pop of color and a sense of zest to any tablescape. Mix and match Chartreuse with colors like White and Turquoise for a lively and summery table setting, or pair it with Plum and Slate for a sophisticated and modern combination.

Notes on Chartreuse

Post 86 Chartreuse ran two years, 1997 to 1999, and it is nothing like the vintage color of the same name. Where the 1951 Chartreuse was a soft yellow-green, this one is genuinely acid - one of the most saturated glazes in the whole line. It arrived the same year as the Raspberry presentation bowls and displaced Sapphire on the shelf. Two years is a short run, so it's not easy to find. Lemongrass, which came along in 2009, is the closest Fiesta has come to repeating it. If you're searching listings, filter by the years - the name alone will give you both eras.

Using Chartreuse on a table

Chartreuse carries enough color to lead a table on its own. Use it for the plates and let quieter pieces do the rest; two or three settings of it go a long way. At L* 80 it is one of the paler glazes in the line: dark food reads sharply against it, pale food much less so. It nearly disappears against a white cloth (1.7:1), so it shows best on dark wood or a colored runner.

Where Chartreuse sits in the line

Chartreuse arrived while Sea Mist Green was still in production, and is noticeably more vivid than it - a clearly different glaze rather than a reformulation (ΔE 22.1). It lasted 2 years where the median for its era is 7, which is why it turns up so rarely. In 1997 it arrived alongside Raspberry and took the place of Sapphire and Raspberry on the shelf.
