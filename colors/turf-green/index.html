Turf Green

Retired

1969–1972

Similar to the color of a fresh cut lawn, meet Turf Green. One of the final colors to be introduced before production stopped on Fiesta in 1972, it was only produced for three years by The Fiesta Tableware Company. Turf Green is a rich and saturated shade of green that is often used in sports fields, golf courses, and other landscapes where a lush, green appearance is desired.

Notes on Turf Green

Turf Green arrived in 1969 with Antique Gold, the last two colors of the original run, and like its partner it only had three years before Fiesta was retired in 1973 — which makes it one of the scarcer vintage colors simply because so little of it was made. The name is accurate: it's a muted, slightly olive green, noticeably more muted than the Medium Green it followed. It reads as very much of its moment, and it's the color that most obviously places a vintage table at the tail end of the original line rather than its middle. Nothing Fiesta makes today is especially close to it.

Using Turf Green on a table

Turf Green has enough color to be noticed without taking over, which makes it a good second or third piece — a salad plate or a serving bowl rather than the whole setting. Its mid lightness (L* 53) means most food reads against it without either washing out or vanishing. It nearly disappears against a white cloth (4.0:1), so it shows best on dark wood or a colored runner.

Where Turf Green sits in the line

Turf Green took over from Medium Green as Fiesta's green, and is noticeably more muted than it — a clearly different glaze rather than a reformulation (ΔE 15.8). It lasted 3 years where the median for its era is 9, which is why it turns up so rarely. In 1969 it arrived alongside Antique Gold and took the place of Yellow, 1936–1969, Turquoise, 1937–1969 and Medium Green on the shelf. What else happened in 1969 →
