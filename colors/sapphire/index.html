Sapphire

Retired

1996–1997 · Color No. 116

Introducing Sapphire, made by The Fiesta Tableware Company from 1996 to 1997. It's a deep and luxurious shade of blue, reminiscent of the precious gemstone it's named after. Sapphire adds a touch of opulence and sophistication to any tablescape. Mix and match Sapphire with colors like White and Shamrock for a classic and elegant table setting, or pair it with Sunflower and Tangerine for a bold and dynamic combination.

Notes on Sapphire

Sapphire had the shortest run of any regular Fiesta color — introduced in 1996, gone in 1997. One year. That alone makes it one of the most sought-after Post 86 colors, and it's the one people are most often surprised to learn existed. It's a deep, genuinely vivid blue, much brighter and more saturated than Cobalt, which is what makes it striking. Lapis, introduced in 2013, is the closest thing Fiesta has made since, though Lapis is calmer. If you see Sapphire listed cheaply, look twice — it's frequently confused with the more common Cobalt.

Using Sapphire on a table

Sapphire carries enough color to lead a table on its own. Use it for the plates and let quieter pieces do the rest; two or three settings of it go a long way. Its mid lightness (L* 33) means most food reads against it without either washing out or vanishing. It separates strongly from a white cloth (8.3:1) and less so from dark wood.

Where Sapphire sits in the line

Sapphire arrived while Periwinkle Blue was still in production, and is darker and more vivid than it — a complete change of direction rather than a reformulation (ΔE 38.2). It lasted 1 year where the median for its era is 7, which is why it turns up so rarely. What else happened at the company in 1996 →
